Look beyond
the call window.

Choosing a communication app also means choosing where account data, messages, and activity records can accumulate. Here is what Discord documents—and what FastCast does differently today.

A documented historical scale

Trillions
of messages.

Discord’s engineering team reported that its message database held trillions of messages by the beginning of 2022. Its explanation was published March 6, 2023.

This is historical, platform-wide message volume—not a current count, a per-user total, or a quantity of recorded calls.

Read Discord’s engineering report ↗

Collection and retention are different questions

What can sit behind an account?

These categories are described in Discord’s privacy policy. Some processing can be limited through its privacy settings.

ACCOUNT

Identity & contact details

Account details such as email, username, birthday, and—in some circumstances—a phone number or verification information.

CONTENT

Messages & uploads

Messages, drafts, files, profiles, and other content you provide through the service.

ACTIVITY

Device & usage information

IP and device information, plus logs about how and when the service is used.

What this does not mean

Discord says it does not sell personal information, generally does not store voice/video call contents, and does not store screen streams apart from a temporary cover thumbnail. It describes voice/video communications as designed for end-to-end encryption. Collection and retention concerns should not be confused with a claim that all calls are recorded. Source: Discord privacy policy.

FastCast’s current scope

A smaller service.
A different data footprint.

The public FastCast preview is a native screen-sharing tool with no FastCast-hosted chat archive or managed media relay. It also does not yet offer Discord’s hosted messaging and community features. A “zero versus trillions” comparison would hide that difference.

Media uses a reachable peer route. Website and download requests go to GitHub; settings and diagnostics can remain locally; a VPN, network provider, or viewing device still belongs in your privacy decisions.
