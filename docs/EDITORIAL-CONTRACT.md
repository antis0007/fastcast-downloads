## Writing requirements

- Identify the product: screen sharing for Windows and Android. Explain which
  device shares and which receives. The home headline is “Share your screen with
  another device.”
- Headings name a subject or task. Do not turn every section into a slogan.
- Each sentence must supply a fact, explanation, action, or relevant qualification.
  Delete redundant sentences instead of paraphrasing them elsewhere on the page.
- Write connected prose where relationships need explanation. Do not split a
  thought into fragments to create emphasis, or lengthen a clear instruction
  merely to avoid short sentences. Labels and button text can be brief.
- Describe the product without praising the honesty, simplicity, or quality of
  its description. State the evidence or limitation itself.
- Keep the independence argument specific: users can share a screen alongside
  their existing chat service; direct media avoids a relay when the route works.
  Do not imply that all connectivity services, accounts, or network costs vanish.
- Keep necessary negatives. Signing warnings, absent features, account scope,
  website tracking disclosures, and unverified test results are useful information.
- Use actual app labels for setup and controls. Describe the viewer-first
  invitation sequence until the released workflow changes.
- Keep the wizard's jokes in its separate voice pool. The footer's deliberate
  brand line can remain; operational prose must be understandable without the theme.
- Apply these rules to metadata, generated tables, captions, accessible labels,
  download descriptions, and README copy as well as visible page paragraphs.
