Who builds Pyrenet,

Pyrenet is private small-group communication and shared computing. This page covers who maintains it, what runs where, and which claims on this site are tested rather than intended.

What we build

Pyrenet is the private small-group communications and shared-computing fabric. Cast is its live-media layer: screen sharing, video, voice, rooms, and file transfer.

Screen and camera media travel directly between your devices when a direct route works, and fall back to a relay that forwards them encrypted when it does not. Screen sharing needs no account at all.

Friends, calls, rooms and file offers pass through a coordination service, which holds your profile, your friend list and your room discussions. Discussion text is readable by that service over authenticated TLS. It is not a hosted archive you can scroll back through, and nothing about your conversations is sold or fed to analytics. Why we build it

Current tools ask you to choose between privacy and convenience. Slack, Discord, Zoom, and Teams host your data on their servers. Self-hosted alternatives are powerful but complex.

Pyrenet takes a third path: a direct route where the network allows one, a relay when it does not, and a free preview with no subscription. The trade-off is stated rather than hidden — the coordination service sees who you talk to and what you type in a room, and the media does not pass through it.

Open source

The native app is dual-licensed under MIT or Apache-2.0. You can read the code, build it, ship modified versions, or just audit the security boundary.

This repository holds the public website and preview packages. Native application source and issue tracking live in the main app repository.

Design principles

No accounts by default. Screen sharing works without any identity. Friends, calls, and rooms use a device-owned keypair that never leaves your hardware.

Encrypted in transit. Media is encrypted on the wire, and a relay forwards ciphertext rather than pictures it can read. End-to-end encryption covers call invitations, not discussion text: the coordination service can read what you type in a room.

Progressive disclosure. The site prefers plain language — invitation, route, permission — over jargon. Technical depth lives behind links, not in the critical path.

Tested before claimed. Every capability has a status word. "Preview" means automated checks pass and hardware testing is still outstanding. "Available" means both.

Team & contact

Maintainers

A single principal developer with AI-assisted review cycles. Human review focuses on networking, permissions, resource lifetimes, security boundaries, and recovery behaviour.

Community

Bugs, feature requests, and design discussion happen in the open. The issue tracker is the primary channel; pull requests are welcome against the app repository.
